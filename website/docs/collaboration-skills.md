# Collaboration Skills for Emotional and Social Moments

Emotional and social literacy often involves group dynamics, trust, boundaries, repair, inclusion, peer pressure, and conflict. Collaboration helps kids make shared work safer, fairer, and more visible.

This page is the local doorway into the Literacy for Kids [Collaboration Toolkit](https://www.literacy-for-kids.com/docs/toolkits/collaboration), connected to the emotional and social skills this curriculum builds.

## A few core ideas

- **Collaboration is not the same as people-pleasing.** A group can work together and still respect "no."
- **Fair group work includes voice, roles, and repair.** Everyone needs a real way to contribute.
- **Boundaries still matter in groups.** Shared work does not cancel personal limits.
- **Unsafe situations need trusted adult help.** Bullying and exclusion are not collaboration problems for kids to fix alone.
- **Inclusion matters** — and no child should be forced to repair exclusion by themselves.

## When this shows up

- When group work feels unfair
- When someone takes over
- When someone is left out
- When a group needs repair
- When boundaries are needed
- When a group dynamic feels unsafe
- When collaboration and peer pressure get tangled

## Tools that help

- **Fair voice check** — has everyone had a real way in?
- **Role check** — visible jobs prevent takeover and exclusion.
- **Group repair check** — what happened, who was affected, what should change?
- **Boundary + collaboration script** — "I want to help, and that role does not work for me."
- **Trusted adult help** — for anything unsafe, targeted, or too big.

:::tip Collaboration Moment
Collaboration does not mean ignoring your boundaries. A group can work together and still respect "no," "I need help," or "that does not feel okay." Safe collaboration includes voice and choice.
:::

:::caution Everyday collaboration skills, not unsafe conflict management
These are everyday collaboration tools, not therapy, bullying intervention, conflict mediation, or a replacement for trusted adults, educators, caregivers, or qualified support. Kids should not be expected to manage unsafe, coercive, bullying, discriminatory, or threatening group situations alone. If a situation feels unsafe, targeted, or too big, involve a trusted adult right away.
:::
